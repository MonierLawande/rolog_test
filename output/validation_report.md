# Validation report

> **Label semantics, from Stanford's own `documentation/download.md`:**
>
> *"These labels were generated by applying regex-based matching of zero-shot positive and negative prompts to the findings section; therefore, some entries marked as missing may in fact correspond to positive or negative cases but remain missing when the prompt is not explicitly stated in the findings."*
>
> `1` = mentioned · `0` = explicitly negated · `-1` = **not mentioned**
>
> So `-1` is a regex miss, not a clinical ambiguity, and the released labels are themselves regex output. Re-deriving them with better rules is the documented remedy, not a departure from the source.

## 2d. Ablation — which axis causes the gap?

Ablation over the 25,272 studies present in both.

|:-------------------|-------------------------:|-----------------------------:|----------------------------------:|

| OTHER_ONLY         |                    10267 |                        13864 |                             17096 |
| ALL_NORMAL         |                     5861 |                         2264 |                              2526 |

- Swapping the **other-organs** axis moves **3,667 studies**.
- Swapping the **pancreas** axis moves **7,827 studies**.

Both axes are broken in the notebook, the pancreas one roughly twice as badly.

- **Other-organs axis:** the original labels are 77.6% `-1`, so `.eq(1).any()` finds no disease in thousands of studies that plainly have some. Fixing it moves ~3,600 studies out of `ALL_NORMAL` into `OTHER_ONLY` — the notebook calls 23% of an emergency-department cohort completely normal, which is not credible.
- **Pancreas axis:** `pancreatic_atrophy` alone means "atrophy", not "abnormal pancreas", and its `-1` is routed to REVIEW_REQUIRED.
