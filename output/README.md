## Primary target — `class_5`

Class names match `Merlin_Analysis.ipynb` (cell 74) exactly.

| class | n | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| OTHER_ONLY | 17,220 | 67.57% |
| PANCREAS_AND_OTHER | 4,998 | 19.61% |
| ALL_NORMAL | 2,559 | 10.04% |
| REVIEW_REQUIRED | 602 | 2.36% |
| **PANCREAS_ONLY** | **107** | **0.42%** |

Built from two axes, both auditable in the same file:

| axis | column | source |
|---|---|---|
| pancreas | `pancreas_status` | 20 `panc_*` concepts, 100% coverage |
| other organs | `other_organs_status` | 29 findings, ≥1 present ⇒ ABNORMAL |

`POSTOPERATIVE` folds into the pancreas-abnormal side for `class_5`, while
`pancreas_status` keeps it as its own value.

### Why the axes differ from the notebook

**The authority is Stanford's own `documentation/download.md`:**

> *"These labels were generated by applying regex-based matching of zero-shot
> positive and negative prompts to the findings section; therefore, some entries
> marked as missing may in fact correspond to positive or negative cases but
> remain missing when the prompt is not explicitly stated in the findings."*
>
> `1` = mentioned · `0` = explicitly negated · `-1` = **not mentioned**

So `-1` is a regex miss, not a clinical ambiguity — and the released labels are
themselves regex output, which makes re-deriving them with better rules the
documented remedy rather than a departure from the source.

The ablation in `validation_report.md` §2d isolates each axis:

| | REVIEW | P_AND_O | P_ONLY | OTHER_ONLY | ALL_NORMAL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V0 notebook as written | 8,412 | 652 | 80 | 10,267 | 5,861 |
| V1 + our other-organs axis | 8,412 | 722 | 10 | 13,868 | 2,260 |
| V2 + our pancreas axis (ours) | 588 | 4,972 | 104 | 17,089 | 2,519 |

**Both axes are broken in the notebook; the pancreas one about twice as badly.**

- **Other-organs axis** (moves ~3,700 studies): the original labels are 77.6%
  `-1`, so `.eq(1).any()` finds no disease in thousands of studies that plainly
  have some. The notebook ends up calling 23% of an ED cohort completely normal.
- **Pancreas axis** (moves ~7,800 studies): `pancreatic_atrophy` means *atrophy*,
  not *abnormal pancreas*. Of the studies with a genuinely abnormal or
  post-operative pancreas, the notebook buries **~84%** in `REVIEW_REQUIRED` and
  calls another 130 outright normal.

> An earlier write-up in this project claimed the other-organs axis contributed
> nothing. That was wrong — it came from a throwaway analysis script whose merge
> used `suffixes=('','_zs')`, so it silently read *our* finding columns instead of
> the original ones. The ablation above is computed from properly isolated inputs.

### Fixed defect: `Pancreas:` swallowing the next organ

`GU:` (262 occurrences) and `GI:` (258) were missing from the section-header
vocabulary, so the pancreas section ran on and absorbed the following organ —
renal and bowel findings scored as pancreatic in **172 studies, 133 of them
wrongly ABNORMAL**. Fixed by adding the abbreviated headers plus an
organ-proximity guard on the organ-agnostic concepts (*mass, cyst, calcification,
stent, atrophy*), which reject a clause that names another organ and never names
the pancreas.

Result: leakage **172 studies → 5**, `ABNORMAL` 4,206 → 4,093. `qa_unknown_headers.csv`
now lists any unrecognised header found inside a pancreas section so this cannot
recur silently; the 29 remaining entries are pancreatic-protocol prose fragments,
one occurrence each.

### ⚠️ `PANCREAS_ONLY` is thin

107 studies, ~18 in the test split — too few for a trustworthy per-class metric.
This is the data, not a extraction defect: a diseased pancreas inside an
otherwise spotless ED abdominal CT is genuinely rare, and 87.7% of studies carry
a positive finding somewhere else.

The axis is tunable in `config.py` without touching any code:

| setting | findings in axis | PANCREAS_ONLY | smallest class |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `OTHER_DISEASE_EXCLUDE_INCIDENTAL = False` *(current)* | 29 | 107 | 107 |
| `OTHER_DISEASE_EXCLUDE_INCIDENTAL = True` | 17 | **~1,000** | **~870** |
| `OTHER_DISEASE_MIN_FINDINGS = 2` | 29 | ~490 | ~480 |

`True` drops 12 near-universal degenerative findings (atherosclerosis, osteopenia,
renal cyst, hepatic steatosis, hiatal hernia, prostatomegaly, coronary and aortic
valve calcification, prior cholecystectomy, atelectasis, anasarca, renal
hypodensities) from counting as "other organ disease".

## Label provenance — the hybrid rule

For each of the 29 findings:

```
final = original          if original ∈ {0, 1}
        extracted         if original == -1  (or the study is missing from zero_shot)
```

Each finding therefore carries four columns:

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `<f>` | final binary label |
| `<f>_source` | `original` or `extracted` |
| `<f>_assertion` | `present` / `absent` / `uncertain` / `historical` / `not_mentioned` |
| `<f>_conflict` | 1 when the original 0/1 contradicts strong text evidence |

Coverage went from a mean of **20.7%** in the original file to **100%**, recovering
**44,522 positives** that were sitting in `-1` cells.

> ⚠️ The hybrid rule keeps the original 0/1 cells untouched, which means it also
> keeps their known errors — **1,033 cells** where the original label contradicts
> explicit report text. They are all flagged in `<f>_conflict`. Set
> `config.OVERRIDE_CONFLICTS = True` and re-run to let the extractor win instead.

## Pancreas multi-label columns

20 columns prefixed `panc_`, each with the full four-state assertion:
`panc_atrophy`, `panc_fatty_replacement`, `panc_pancreatitis`,
`panc_pancreatitis_acute`, `panc_pancreatitis_chronic`, `panc_necrosis`,
`panc_pseudocyst`, `panc_cystic_lesion`, `panc_ipmn`, `panc_solid_mass`,
`panc_malignancy`, `panc_duct_dilation`, `panc_calcification`,
`panc_peripancreatic_inflam`, `panc_divisum_annular`, `panc_stent`,
`panc_transplant`, `panc_postop_resection`, `panc_explicit_normal`,
`panc_no_abnormality`, `panc_not_evaluated`.

Unlike the 29 findings, these are matched **only inside the pancreas context**
(explicit `Pancreas:` section, pancreas-bearing sentences in free prose, and
pancreas-bearing impression sentences). Words like *mass*, *cyst*, *atrophy* and
*calcification* are organ-agnostic; matching them over the whole report is what
made earlier passes count renal and muscular atrophy as pancreatic.

Context provenance: 22,207 from an explicit section, 3,231 from free prose,
48 with no pancreas mention at all → `REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

## Caveats you must not lose

1. **No `patient_id` exists.** 25,494 scans come from 18,317 patients, so ~7,177
   are repeat visits with nothing to group on. **Do not build your own splits** —
   keep the official `Split` column. Three byte-identical report pairs that
   straddled train/val have already been removed.
2. **`fy_*` columns are passed through unchanged.** The `0/1/2/3` encoding is
   undocumented upstream and is *not* ordinal — mean age by value runs
   47.3 → 58.6 → 64.0 → **57.5** for `cvd`. Check the Merlin repo before using.
3. **QC columns are flags, not filters.** `slice_ok`, `phase_portal`,
   `metadata_contradiction` (1,519 scans tag `contrast=False` yet
   `phase=portal_venous`), `age_implausible` (365 under 18, 20 over 100).
   The cohort decision is yours.
4. **`has_metadata=0` for 77 studies** and `has_zero_shot=0` for 214.
5. **Rule-based labels are not clinical ground truth.** Automated validation only
   was run, per project decision — no human-annotated sample exists. Agreement
   against the original labels ranges 89–100% per finding.
6. **`duplicate_conflict=1`** marks the 2 studies whose colliding `study_id` had
   two genuinely different reports. Their original zero-shot labels were computed
   from an unknown one of the two texts; treat those rows with suspicion.

## Class imbalance

63% of reports describe a literally normal pancreas. Mass/neoplasm sits at ~1.2%
prevalence — roughly 65 positives in the test split, which is **too few for a
standalone pancreatic-cancer detector**. Atrophy, pancreatitis and ductal
dilation are the realistic targets.
